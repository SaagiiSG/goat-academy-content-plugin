# Frameworks: Jun Yuh

Built from the six verified note batches covering S001 to S034, weighted to the content-ideas lane. 25 frameworks, roughly most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that teach or apply the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. He teaches full-time creators, so each entry carries an **Owner version**: how a busy owner (shop, agency, service provider, coach) runs it in 30 to 60 minutes a week. Owner versions and How-to-score lines are council guidance built from his stated numbers, not quotes.

---

## 1. Message plus content pillars (his replacement for a niche)
- **What it is:** One overarching message (he also calls it an ethos) with three or four content pillars under it. Every idea gets filed under a pillar, so posts stay on-brand without boxing you into one topic.
- **How the pillars changed over time:** Dec 2025: three interests plus your story (S018). Feb 2026: skill, mindset, lifestyle, passions (S015). Apr 2026: skills, perspective, lifestyle (S011). Jul 2026: skills, passions, story (S007). **Newest, Oct 2026:** the storytelling wheel areas (love, identity, fitness, earnings) used as four pillars, each told from your own perspective (S001). The constant across every version: one pillar is the skill you sell and one is your story (inferred from S001, S007, S011, S015, S018).
- **Steps:** (1) Find the message with his three questions: do I hold a contrarian view, what change do I want my audience to experience, where do people already turn to me for help (S011, S015). (2) If stuck, ask friends, family or clients what they come to you for (S019, S020). (3) Pick three or four pillars. (4) List topics under each pillar.
- **Owner version:** Message = the change you create for customers. Pillars = your craft (how you do the work), your thinking (opinions your industry gets wrong), your story (why you started, what you got wrong). One 30-minute sitting gives a list of 30 topics; each week draw one per pillar.
- **When to use:** Setup, or when posts feel random or are all service tips.
- **How to score:** Strong if every post maps to a pillar and the story pillar shows up at least weekly. Weak if every post is a tip, which he calls "the same thing that every other expert in the space is doing." (S004)
- **Quote:** "Three of those content pillars should be interests of yours. And then that fourth is your story." (S018)
- **Quote:** "do I have a contrarian view? Does my industry or does society say one thing and do I believe another?" (S015)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S007, S011, S015, S018, S019, S020, S029, S030)

## 2. Creator Vision (what, who, why) *(he also says "Creative Vision")*
- **What it is:** A one-page map that answers "what do I post?" Your name sits at the top with branches beneath it; content starters fall out at the bottom.
- **Parts:** WHAT = message plus pillars. WHO = target avatar, demographic plus psychographic (pains and desires), with psychographic weighted more. WHY = your stories told with problem, pursuit, payoff.
- **How it changed:** Nov 2025 (S020) and Jan 2026 (S030) call the third branch "uniqueness" (pain, experience, skill set). Feb to Apr 2026 (S015, S012, S011) call it "why". **Newer dated label is why.** The undated S032 interview adds a fourth branch, monetization.
- **Steps:** (1) Write the three branches. (2) Under each, list concrete items (pains, desires, stories). (3) Run hook templates (framework 9) against the bottom items to get starters. (4) Put the starters in the content bank.
- **Owner version:** WHO is your best paying customer before they hired you: what they lie awake about and what they want. Fill this once in 45 minutes; revisit quarterly.
- **When to use:** "I don't know what to post", or ideas feel off-brand.
- **How to score:** Guidance: the owner can produce 20 content starters from the bottom branches in one sitting. If they cannot name the customer's pains in the customer's words, the WHO branch is empty.
- **Quote:** "You'll never run out of content ideas if you have the creative vision and you just have a series of hook templates" (S011)
- **Quote:** "every one thinks about demographic but I obsess over psychographic" (S032)
- **Sources:** 8 (S010, S011, S012, S015, S020, S029, S030, S032)

## 3. Problem, Pursuit, Payoff (the three Ps, 3P)
- **What it is:** The spine of every post that sells. Problem makes people care, pursuit is your method (and becomes your offer), payoff shows it works.
- **Steps:** (1) Problem: a specific struggle, stated first. (2) Pursuit: what you actually did; never skip it. (3) Payoff: the outcome or insight; an unresolved ending still counts. Proportions in his example: problem 7 lines, pursuit 7, payoff 5, then reflection and a call to action; he says the ratio and order matter, not the count (S006).
- **Finding your pursuit (three prompts, S004):** what did I do repeatedly that I stopped noticing; what would I tell a friend to do step by step; what did I get wrong first.
- **Owner version:** Service provider format from S004: show a common mistake, your method, then the better result. Physical products: show the product solving a real problem on a real person, and sell the feeling: "you're not selling jewelry. You are selling confidence." (S004)
- **When to use:** Every post, carousel and script.
- **How to score:** Fail if the pursuit is missing (it "feels more like a flex than it does actually helpful content" (S006)), if the problem is vague (just saying you struggled), or if success fills most of the runtime.
- **Quote:** "Your problem made somebody care. Your payoff showed that it would work for them. The pursuit is now the method you sell." (S004)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S006, S011, S012, S014, S015, S020, S030)

## 4. Repurposing ladder *(unnamed; "post every day without creating every day")*
- **What it is:** One shoot or idea feeds many posts, so posting frequency is decoupled from creation time.
- **Parts:** (1) One source video into seven posts: vlog, green screen reaction, lesson carousel, caption video, silent film, split screen, story frames (S013). (2) Same B-roll, new message, sound, trend or format (S017, S019, S023). (3) Repost winners. (4) Derivatives: keep a winning format but change the visual frame and the narrative (S030). (5) Double down on the format and messaging of anything that works (S015).
- **How reposting changed:** May 2023: repost content from three weeks prior, changing the hook or clip order (S024). **Newer, Jan 2026 and S032:** repost the exact video after about two months; and Meta now catches reposts that only change the hook (S030).
- **Owner version:** One 30-minute shoot of a normal workday becomes a week: a carousel of the steps, a caption video, a before-and-after, three Stories. Keep a list of winners and repost each one after two months.
- **When to use:** No time to create; a post did well; a week with nothing new.
- **How to score:** Guidance: one shoot should yield at least a week of posts. If every post needs a fresh shoot, the system is broken.
- **Quote:** "What if I told you that you can post every day without having to create every day?" (S013)
- **Quote:** "I always give my videos 2 months before I repost a video because it gives a new life while not feeling redundant" (S032)
- **Sources:** 8 (S013, S015, S017, S019, S023, S024, S030, S032)

## 5. Weekly intensity mix and content planner *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Plan each week by energy cost, not just topic, so you never burn out and never miss a post.
- **Steps:** (1) Planner with three rows: idea, format, intensity (S015), plus dates (S020). (2) Rate intensity. (3) Mix mostly low with one or two higher-effort posts. (4) On depleted days, use the lowest format (caption video) rather than skip (S013, S016).
- **Thresholds and how they changed:** Dec 2025: low is "sub 30 minutes of editing", with four to five low plus two medium to high a week (S017). Feb 2026: under an hour is low, five low plus one or two medium to high (S015). Newer definition is looser.
- **Owner version:** Floor of three posts a week (S018, S029): two low (single B-roll with text, a Story poll answer) and one higher (a talking video on a customer question). Schedule each by its time cost, as he does (S032).
- **When to use:** Weekly planning; any week the owner says they have no time.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a week planned with no low-intensity slot is a week that will be missed.
- **Quote:** "all I do for my content planner is have three rows. The first row is idea, the second row is format, and the third row is intensity." (S015)
- **Quote:** "Because this piece of content took me about 15 minutes to make and it got 11.9 million views." (S019)
- **Sources:** 7 (S013, S015, S016, S017, S019, S020, S030)

## 6. Silent film storytelling format
- **What it is:** His top format: a story told only with B-roll, on-screen text and music. No talking on camera.
- **Steps (S014):** (1) Hook with pain: text naming a specific pain plus a clip where you look less happy (it need not be from that moment). (2) Add a timestamp or time anchor (a year, month or age). (3) Make the text readable: read each line aloud and cut the frame right after. (4) Use a sound with rising action and a drop, even an old one. (5) One theme per video, never a biography. The arc inside is pain, pursuit, payoff.
- **Owner version:** Ideal for camera-shy owners: a year-anchored line about the hard early days, over workday clips. Ten a month is his stated target for story videos (S014).
- **When to use:** Reach posts, owners who will not talk on camera, muted viewers.
- **How to score:** Guidance: frames you cannot read aloud before they change fail; "the ones that are bad are the ones that have like 18 words on one frame." (S030) He admits "it's not 100% success rate." (S030)
- **Quote:** "It does not require you to speak in front of the camera. It solely relies on B-roll, text, and music, which all of you guys can do." (S014)
- **Sources:** 7 (S006, S012, S013, S014, S015, S020, S030)

## 7. Evaluate loop: 20 by 5, weekly averages, one variable *(his 100 video rule, revised)*
- **What it is:** Judge content by batches, not single posts, then change one thing at a time.
- **Steps:** (1) Post 20 without limiting yourself; (2) find the top 20%; (3) repeat five times (S032). (4) Take weekly average views; if two weeks in a row drop, check retention or skip rate (S017). (5) Change one variable at a time (S003). (6) Keep 80% proven formats, 20% experiments (S030).
- **How it changed:** May 2023: make 100 videos in a beginner mindset before judging (S025), and "literally never look at my own numbers" (S023). **Newer, Dec 2025 onward:** weekly averages (S017) and the 20 by 5 version (S032).
- **Owner version:** Every Friday, five minutes: note the week's average views and the one post that beat it; next week, make one more post like it.
- **When to use:** "Is this working?"; after a flop; deciding what to repeat.
- **How to score:** Guidance: never judge one post. Two down weeks means look at the first seconds (hook), not the topic.
- **Quote:** "I actually have my own version of The 100 video Rule and that you post 20 videos without limiting yourself you understand the top 20% perform videos and you repeat that five times" (S032)
- **Quote:** "I always think like 80% should be proven formats, 20% could be experimentation" (S030)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S017, S023, S025, S030, S032, S034)

## 8. Format study: 3 by 3 and 7 by 7
- **What it is:** Ideas are rarely the problem; packaging is. Study how top posts are built, then put your message in that structure.
- **Steps:** (1) Pick three creators (S019) or seven (S020, S030), from any industry. (2) Take three (or seven) top performers from each. (3) Break each down: hook, visuals and editing, cadence, audio, story arc, CTA (S020). (4) Take the framework, not the video, and merge it with your message and audience (S032).
- **Owner version:** 3 by 3 once a month in 30 minutes: nine posts, one note line each. His 7 by 7 (49 posts every two weeks) is a full-time creator load. He says about four weeks of study changes the lens for good (S030).
- **When to use:** Posts are not working; picking a new format.
- **How to score:** Guidance: the owner can name the format of their last three posts. If the answer is that it was relatable, he says that is not objective (S015).
- **Quote:** "No one owns a format. Everyone owns a message and that's what's unique to them." (S019)
- **Quote:** "you take the framework of it you combine it with your own unique message your target audience your unique dis value and your monetization routes" (S032)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S015, S019, S020, S030, S032)

## 9. Hook templates as idea starters
- **What it is:** Fill-in-the-blank openers run against each pillar and pain. The permutations are the idea bank.
- **Templates (verbatim):** "I used to believe blank until this happened." (S011) "Success in blank has less to do with talent and more to do with blank." (S011) "If you think it's impossible to balance both creating content and running a business, here's the batching method that I would recommend." (S015) "Here are five of the biggest mistakes you're making when it comes to keeping your space clean." (S019) "If you keep opening Instagram with no idea what to post, you need to watch this." (S003)
- **Five hook types (S019):** say why you are an expert, ask a question, positive statement, negative or controversial statement, reference something recognizable, target a specific audience.
- **Owner version:** Write five templates on one card. Each week, fill each with one customer pain: five ideas in ten minutes.
- **When to use:** Blank page; turning a topic into a post.
- **How to score:** Guidance: the hook names a specific audience or pain; generic hooks ("I learned something crazy") fail his test (S003).
- **Quote:** "the amount of permutations that you can generate is absurd" (S011)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S011, S012, S015, S019, S030)

## 10. Audience mining and cheap validation *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Ideas come from what the audience asks and reacts to; test them cheaply before producing.
- **Steps:** (1) Collect comments, DMs, Story replies and live questions (S024). (2) Go one level deeper: ask why something helped or did not (S032). (3) Call engaged followers and write down what they are going through (S032). (4) Test the idea in its basic form with a Story poll or live topic before spending hours on it (S032). (5) With no audience yet, read competitors' comments for gaps (S029).
- **Owner version:** Owners already sit on this bank: sales calls, intake forms, emails, reviews. Every customer question asked twice is a post. Keep a running list; one Story poll a week validates the next post.
- **When to use:** Choosing between ideas; planning an offer; stuck for topics.
- **How to score:** Guidance: an idea with a poll response, repeated DM or repeated customer question beats one the owner just likes.
- **Quote:** "I need something low effort because I'm busy so I posted a story of like a simple poll poll and survey" (S032)
- **Quote:** "Is there a missing gap that you could potentially take advantage of?" (S029)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S010, S022, S024, S029, S032)

## 11. Batching in stages *(he calls it pre-production, production, post-production)*
- **What it is:** Script, film and edit in separate blocks, never all on one day.
- **Steps:** (1) Script in one sitting. (2) Film in another. (3) Edit in a third (S017). His Jan 2026 schedule: scripts Saturday afternoon, filming Tuesday morning, editing Thursday (S030). In 2023 it was one Saturday-morning block of about three hours (S023). (4) At minimum, batch the brainstorming (S025). (5) Batch about one week ahead, not a month (S017, S023).
- **Owner version:** One 45-minute block: 15 minutes to pick three ideas and write bullet scripts, 20 to film, 10 to queue. Editing can be handed off.
- **When to use:** Owner says content eats the week.
- **How to score:** Guidance: if scripting, filming and editing happen the same day, expect frustration and missed posts (S013).
- **Quote:** "I like to script my content in one sitting and then I like to film my content in another and I like to edit my content in the third block throughout the week." (S017)
- **Quote:** "it should be about a week proactively. It shouldn't be more than that because content changes all the time" (S017)
- **Sources:** 6 (S013, S017, S020, S023, S025, S030)

## 12. B-roll bank, scene library and home base
- **What it is:** A stock of your own clips, sorted by scene, so most posts are assembled rather than filmed.
- **Steps:** (1) One day a week, take your phone through a normal routine (S025). (2) Film five to six clips per category, 10 to 12 seconds each, in one two-hour block, labeled in phone albums (S017); or 5 to 15 second clips in three angles: wide, close-up, POV (S013). (3) Tag by scene action, his "scene library" (S030). (4) Pick a home base: one scene you can film in five minutes with predictable results (S023, S025). (5) Film emotions today to use under old stories (S006).
- **Owner version:** Categories = the shop or studio, the work in progress, the finished result, you at the desk, customers (with permission). One hour gives a month of B-roll.
- **When to use:** Setup; whenever filming feels like the bottleneck.
- **How to score:** Guidance: if the owner films fresh footage for every post, they have no bank.
- **Quote:** "you can get enough B-roll for a full month's worth of content." (S017)
- **Quote:** "it will take me five minutes to create a clip if I'm using my desk as my home base" (S023)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S013, S017, S023, S025, S030)

## 13. Talking plus non-talking formats (the format menu)
- **What it is:** Two format families working together. Talking video builds expertise, depth and trust; non-talking (B-roll, text, music, carousels, silent film) is easier to optimize for reach (S017).
- **Menu:** His six formats of 2026: silent film, split screen reel, advice I needed at X age, old me versus new me, the problem statement, split screen carousel (S006). Plus caption video, green screen reaction, lesson carousel (S013). Short pieces can drive viewers to a 1.5 to 2 minute long-form (S012).
- **Owner version:** Start with non-talking if camera is the blocker, add one talking video a week on a customer question. Many viewers watch muted, so non-talking widens reach (S003, S030).
- **When to use:** Picking a format for an idea; a camera-shy owner.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a week with zero talking posts lacks trust-building; a week with only talking posts is hard to sustain.
- **Quote:** "Number one, a talking video. And number two, a non-talking video." (S017)
- **Quote:** "they're much more consistent to get good viewership if you do them right" (S006)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S006, S012, S013, S017, S030)

## 14. Read your life: moment to idea workflow and content bank *(four-step idea workflow, unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Nothing to post is a seeing problem, not a life problem. Log real moments, extract meaning, multiply with angles, store in one bank.
- **Steps (S002):** (1) Write down something you did in the last week, however boring. (2) Look inside the moment: the reaction, choice or surprise that made you pause. (3) Ask why it stuck and what it made you realize; keep asking why to chain more moments. (4) Run four angles: memory, tension, usefulness, change; skip any angle that would be forced. (5) Save it in one bank. Related angle set in S004: run one event mentally, spiritually and physically, each through its own three Ps.
- **Capture habits:** phone list of what he is experiencing (S028), a physical journal (S001), and extracting ideas every morning before scrolling (S030).
- **Owner version:** Friday, 10 minutes: three moments from the workweek (a client question, a mistake, a decision) into the bank, each with one angle. His business prompt: "Think about why you've even chosen that career path." (S002)
- **When to use:** "My business is boring"; nothing to post.
- **How to score:** Guidance: if the owner freezes when asked for a story, he says they "actually haven't done this practice well enough" (S002).
- **Quote:** "it's not about living a more interesting life or doing more things. It's about recounting in different ways the things that you've already undergone." (S002)
- **Quote:** "now you have four video ideas from one moment in your life." (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S004, S028, S030)

## 15. Story mining: LIFE wheel, three whys, transformation list
- **What it is:** Tools for finding the stories behind the posts.
- **Steps:** (1) Storytelling wheel: love (relationships, hobbies), identity (origin, culture, faith), fitness (mind, body, spirit), earnings (career, clients, skills) (S001, S006). (2) Take any moment and ask why three times: the obvious reason, the feeling underneath, the part you do not say aloud; that takes a story from safe to real to raw (S006, S028). (3) Transformation list: open a doc, set a 30-minute timer, list 20 or more transformative experiences, then ask a chatbot to group the themes (S014, S029).
- **Owner version:** The earnings slice is where business stories live: failing business to thriving one, first client, the hire that went wrong. One 30-minute list feeds a quarter of story posts.
- **When to use:** Story pillar is empty; owner only posts service tips.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a story that stops at the first why is safe and generic; the third why is what lands.
- **Quote:** "These each layers are going to be the obvious reason then the feeling underneath and then the part you don't say aloud." (S006)
- **Quote:** "All you have to do is open up a Google doc and start a 30 minute timer next to you." (S014)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S006, S014, S028, S029)

## 16. Younger You principle
- **What it is:** When you do not know who to talk to or what to say, target yourself two to three years younger. You remember their pains and can teach the transformation without outside research.
- **Steps:** (1) Describe that person. (2) List what they struggled with. (3) Ask what you would tell them. (4) Turn each answer into a framework or a hook.
- **Owner version:** Younger you is the owner before they learned the trade, or the typical client at intake (inferred from S017, S029). His age range varies: two to three years (S003, S020, S029), three (S027).
- **When to use:** Writing hooks; choosing topics; defining the WHO branch.
- **How to score:** Guidance: the hook would have stopped the younger you mid-scroll.
- **Quote:** "If you were to talk to your 2 to 3-year younger version of yourself, what would you say? What would you need to tell them? What struggles were they experiencing?" (S029)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S017, S020, S027, S029)

## 17. Dictate, then let AI find patterns *(unnamed; AI as blind-spot finder)*
- **What it is:** Talk ideas out first, then use AI to clean up, trim and spot patterns. Never to generate ideas or write the story.
- **Steps:** (1) Voice dictate a long narrative (Whisper, Claude, ChatGPT) (S001, S030). (2) Ask AI to remove fluff; park cut paragraphs for another video (S030). (3) Or record a long voice message of your assumptions about your audience and ask for blind spots (S029). (4) Write the final script in your own words (S001, S006).
- **Owner version:** Driving between jobs: a five-minute voice memo on one customer question. AI trims it into a script outline and lists two more topics hiding inside it.
- **When to use:** Blank page; long rambling ideas; checking assumptions about customers.
- **How to score:** Guidance: fail if AI produced the idea or the script; that is the user he says will "fall flat very quickly" (S030).
- **Quote:** "I use it for the one thing that I can't do alone, which is to see what I'm standing too close to." (S001)
- **Quote:** "most people are using AI in the sense of, hey, Chad, can you give me 10 content ideas that fit this space, right?" (S030)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S006, S026, S029, S030)

## 18. Four missions of content: attract, nurture, position, convert (3-1-2-1)
- **What it is:** Every post gets one job. Attract = reach (story, a proven format with your message). Nurture = real life in real time, no transformation needed. Position = credibility (teaching, expert or journey POV). Convert = the offer.
- **Steps:** Assign a mission to each planned post; for seven weekly Reels run three attract, one nurture, two position, one convert (S029, S030). His Dec 2025 simpler version: at least three posts a week, one storytelling, one value, one reach (S018).
- **Owner version:** For a three-post week: one attract (story), one position (process or customer question), and alternate nurture and convert weeks. Owners already convert well; he says they lack top-of-funnel attention (S006).
- **When to use:** Weekly planning; content that gets views but no sales, or sales posts that get no views.
- **How to score:** Guidance: more than one convert post a week is too many; judge convert posts over a month, not alone (S030).
- **Quote:** "So three attract, one nurture, two position, and one convert on a weekly basis for your reels." (S030)
- **Quote:** "And those three are going to be number one storytelling, number two, a value post, and number three a reach post." (S018)
- **Sources:** 4 (S010, S018, S029, S030)

## 19. Journey POV versus expert POV
- **What it is:** Two valid ways to position. Expert POV teaches from results you have; journey POV documents getting there, mistakes included. Faking expertise fails.
- **Steps:** Pick the POV that is true for this topic; write the hook to match. His pairs: "I've been posting every single day for the last six years and here are the six things that I've learned" (S030) versus journey hooks about what is not working yet (S030).
- **Owner version:** Owners usually have expert POV on their craft; use journey POV for anything new (a new service, hiring, a second location).
- **When to use:** Position posts; new offers.
- **How to score:** Guidance: credibility breaks when the POV claims results the owner does not have (S032).
- **Quote:** "So I think whenever you're thinking about content, it's journey or expert POV for your position content and they both work perfectly fine." (S030)
- **Quote:** "create content about the struggles experienced with creating that type of offer when I've never done it before" (S032)
- **Sources:** 4 (S010, S018, S030, S032)

## 20. Hook mechanics: four viewer questions, stacking, layering, payoff
- **What it is:** How to build the first seconds and keep the promise.
- **Parts (S003):** about 1.7 seconds before a swipe; answer the viewer's four questions (is this for me, why care now, what do I get, do I trust you); stack hooks (transformation, credibility, stakes, relation points); open a curiosity gap; align what they hear, see and read; rehook with a question, example or visual change; every section must advance, prove or resolve the hook. Spoken, visual and text hooks can be stacked (S019); the text hook should name a specific pain (S014).
- **Pre-post check:** would the first seconds make sense on mute; does the text add clarity rather than repeat every word (S003).
- **Owner version:** Before posting, run the mute test and ask: would my customer know in two seconds this is for them?
- **When to use:** Scripting; diagnosing high skip rate.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a clickbait hook with a weak payoff loses more than it gains.
- **Quote:** "Your hook earns attention and the rest of your video earns trust." (S003)
- **Quote:** "Number one, would the first few seconds make sense on mute?" (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S014, S019)

## 21. Selfish to selfless flip
- **What it is:** Take content about you (a vlog of your day) and reframe it around the viewer's payoff.
- **Steps:** (1) Take the raw clip or day. (2) Ask what the audience gets. (3) Rewrite the text hook as the viewer's outcome. (4) Add the lesson or list on top (S013).
- **Owner version:** "A day at my bakery" becomes a lesson for the customer's own life or problem.
- **When to use:** Behind-the-scenes posts that get no response.
- **How to score:** Guidance: the hook names a viewer outcome, not your activity.
- **Quote:** "A selfless piece of content is think about what is the payoff for the audience." (S018)
- **Quote:** "Here's how I find time for myself, even as a busy mom." (S018)
- **Sources:** 3 (S013, S018, S032)

## 22. Owner story content: origin, process, why choose you *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** His specific advice to business owners: lead with the founder, not the product, and show why the process works the way it does.
- **Parts:** origin content on why you started (S004); the reasons behind your process and protocols (S006); the differentiation question (S006); personal brand traffic funnels into the business page (S006). If starting from a business page, about three personal-founder pieces to four client or process pieces (S006).
- **Owner version:** Monthly: one origin post, one "why we do it this way" post, one common-mistake post. The business page can run on testimonials and a few posts a month; his own company page gets about five posts a month from him (S030).
- **When to use:** Owners posting only product shots or service menus.
- **How to score:** Guidance: if the feed could belong to any competitor in town, it fails the differentiation question.
- **Quote:** "if I searched up wedding planner on Instagram, I'm going to see 250 wedding planners in my local region. Why the heck would I choose you?" (S006)

## 23. Building in public and the launch cycle
- **What it is:** Before selling anything new, post the build weekly and ask for feedback, so demand and proof exist on launch day.
- **Steps:** (1) Weekly, post one of five: a day in the life, money invested, research, progress, mistakes. (2) Ask for feedback on features, changes, prices, launch date, design. (3) Screenshot real DMs as proof. (4) Launch over about four days with carousel, Stories, email and Reel. (5) After launch, collect reviews and say publicly that you sold out (S010).
- **Owner version:** One build post a week for a month before a new service or product; Story polls on price or name.
- **When to use:** New offer, menu item, location or program.
- **How to score:** Guidance: low-ticket launches can use urgency and shout-outs; high-ticket needs ongoing proof of client results (S010).
- **Quote:** "I recommend once per week on minimum that you do one of these five things that's behind me. Either a day in life, how much money has been invested, research, progress, or mistakes." (S010)
