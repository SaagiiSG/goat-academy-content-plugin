# Stories: Jun Yuh

Built from the batch notes on all 34 sources. 20 stories and cases he reuses, weighted to content ideas for business owners, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses. Dollar and follower figures are his own and vary between tellings.

1. **200 view jail.** He posted fashion and fitness daily from his parents' basement for over a year, spending hours a video for a few hundred views, then talked to the phone about his own struggle as a student and the comments changed from fire emojis to messages saying it changed them. Lesson: a topic copied from others does not grow; your own problem does (S004, S006, S019, S022, S028, S029, S030, S032).
2. **The grocery shop and his dad's favorite food.** Saying he went shopping is empty, but reaching for his dad's favorite food without thinking becomes one post and then three more through memory, usefulness and change. Lesson: one mundane moment, read closely, gives four posts (S002).
3. **47 notebooks.** He tracked formats, angles and hooks across 47 notebooks to see what drove engagement and double down on it. Lesson: ideas come from a logged system, not inspiration (S004).
4. **The coach and the permanent makeup artist.** Felicity, a coach stuck at 54,000 followers, got 10 high-ticket clients once she told her own story; Michelle, a permanent makeup artist, went from 24,000 to 62,000 by showing a common client mistake plus personal stories. Lesson: service providers win on story plus the mistake they fix, not tips (S004).
5. **The jewelry designer and the dad coach.** Valyria had ten failed accounts before story-led posts got 121,000 followers; Boxy dropped workout tips for his story as a burnt-out new dad and made 20,000 AUD in coaching. Lesson: sell the feeling and the founder, not the product shot (S004).
6. **250 wedding planners.** Search wedding planner locally and you see 250; the only answer to why you is your process and why you make each decision. Lesson: process-behind-the-work posts are the differentiator for a local service (S006).
7. **The moving company out of 34 calls.** He picked a mover because he liked the person, not the features. Lesson: trust decides, so show the person (S006).
8. **Guide to Academic Success.** He asked his audience on stories twice a week for about three months, tested with polls, let followers shape the cover and sections, and screenshotted their DMs; the PDF made about $30,000 in its first week. Lesson: customer questions are the product roadmap and the launch proof (S010, S018, S029, S030, S032).
9. **The clothing brand that got crickets.** He floated a clothing line to his audience and heard nothing back, so he did not build it. Lesson: validate cheaply before you produce (S018, S029).
10. **The challenge from an ice cream in Nice.** Fans in France asked for a cheaper way in; one suggested a challenge, he polled stories, built it in public for 38 days, and 4,000 people from 106 countries joined. Lesson: listen for requests in casual conversations (S006, S026).
11. **Ten clips at the desk.** For his first year he reused the same 10 B-roll clips of himself working at his desk, posting daily without creating daily; the desk became his home base after lighting kept ruining planned shoots. Lesson: one reliable scene and a clip bank make daily posting cheap (S017, S023, S025).
12. **The plane post.** Tired on a flight, he filmed a screen of dense text about his own travel journey; it got about a million views in 48 hours. A 15-minute video elsewhere got 11.9 million. Lesson: a low-effort post that hits the audience's pain beats production (S019, S032).
13. **Near-identical reposts.** He showed the same format and message reposted for 22.9 million, 32.8 million and 4.4 million views. Lesson: when something works, repeat it without guilt (S015).
14. **The long voice message to AI.** He recorded a very long voice message of his assumptions about his audience and asked AI for blind spots; it showed people fear being seen more than they want to be creators, so he shifted to communication, psychology and business angles and sales rose. Lesson: use AI on your own thinking, not for ideas (S028, S029).
15. **Losing two million followers on purpose.** His co-founder told him to accept dropping from about 5.3 million toward 3.2 million to move from students to creators and entrepreneurs; Creator College did about $3.6 million in nine months. Lesson: judge content by business results, not follower count (S030).
16. **The mentorship that kept selling.** An old program kept getting sign-ups months after he removed the bio links and stopped emailing. Lesson: attract, nurture, position, convert content plants seeds that sell later (S030).
17. **130 buyers closed in DMs.** He closed each of the first three cohorts of about 130 people himself in DMs before writing a sales video. Lesson: talk to buyers directly first; that is where the words for content come from (S030).
18. **Ed from MIT.** A 40-year-old who never used Instagram grew from zero to over 100,000 followers in four months with one optimized format. Lesson: it is still early, and a proven format beats talent or age (S030).
19. **His worst video.** He sometimes thinks a video is his worst and his team reports it ranked highest. Lesson: you misjudge your own work, so post and test instead of prejudging (S002, S031).
20. **The New York event.** His first big in-person event lost money (he gives $37,000, $49,000 and $50,000 in different tellings) because he pushed it past his team and got pricing and sponsors wrong; London then sold 1,300 seats. Lesson: he learns by doing and owns the mistake, but his impatience costs money (S026, S028, S029).
